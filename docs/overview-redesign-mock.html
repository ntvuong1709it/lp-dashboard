Overview redesign

Four ways to rebuild the Overview page around charts. D combines your picks from B and A. Each uses the app's real colours, fonts and header, and the earnings chart works (Day, Week, Month, and the chart tabs). The numbers are example data, not yours. Needs you, Ready to claim, Allocation and Borrow coverage appear in each option. Pools, Earnings, Positions and Borrow pages are unchanged here.

D · B with A's positions

Option B's top half (warning line, number strip, earnings and portfolio value charts side by side), then A's position cards in two columns, with a column beside them holding Borrow and Allocation by position. Ready to claim lives in the warning line.

Trade-off: the cards are narrower (two per row, three rows), so a position's range bar is shorter than in A.

A · Earnings first

The earnings chart is the page's hero, with Needs you and Borrow in a column beside it. Every position gets a range card underneath, so in range, near edge and out of range are visible at a glance.

Trade-off: the portfolio value chart drops to the bottom, and range cards take the most vertical space of the three options.

B · Two charts

Earnings and portfolio value sit side by side at equal weight, with a single warning line above them instead of a Needs you card. Positions become a dense range table, one row each.

Trade-off: both charts are half-width, so each shows fewer days. The table is the most compact way to scan many positions.

C · One chart, positions beside it

One large chart you switch between Earned, Value and P/L. The positions list sits beside it, sorted worst first, so anything out of range is at the top and Needs you merges into that list. The number cards shrink to one line.

Trade-off: you see one chart at a time. This is the calmest page and the least like today's Overview.

Example data: 6 open positions worth $48,320, one loan. Chart tooltips on bars show the day and amounts. Real values come from the same sources as today's pages.
